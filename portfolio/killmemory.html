REMEMBER WHY SOMETHING FAILED · A MOBLUEHQ PRODUCT

Remember why something failed without suppressing a genuinely new variant. KillMemory keeps a ledger of past rejections with their specific reasons, and checks new submissions against those reasons before deciding whether the same objection still applies.

WHAT IT DOES

Every rejection is stored with the specific reason it failed, who decided that, and a pointer to the evidence behind it — never a bare “no.”

A new submission that looks similar to a past rejection is narrowed to a candidate match by surface-text similarity first — that similarity alone never decides the outcome.

The original, structured reason for the past rejection is then re-checked against the new submission's own details. Only if that specific reason still holds is the new submission treated as an echo of the old one.

Where there isn't enough information to tell whether the original reason still applies, KillMemory doesn't guess in either direction — it reports that it cannot conclude.

A human can grant a named exception with a stated reason. Nothing overrides a recorded rejection silently.

EVIDENCE AND REFUSALS
ECHO

The new submission was checked against the original rejection's specific reason, and that reason still applies.

NEW_VARIANT

The submission looks similar on the surface, but the original rejection's specific reason no longer applies.

CANNOT-CONCLUDE

There isn't enough information to tell whether the original reason still holds; KillMemory declines to guess.

STATUS

KillMemory has not shipped a public build, and has no user interface yet — what exists today is the underlying record-keeping and matching logic.

No suppression-rate or accuracy numbers are published for this tool. “Request access” adds you to the list for when a pilot opens; it does not start a download or a purchase.
