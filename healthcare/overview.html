Overview

The missing layer between a decision and the record that defends it

Healthcare decisions are getting easier to automate and harder to defend after the fact — in an appeal, an audit, or a dispute. The missing artifact usually isn't a smarter answer. It's a defensible record of the process that produced the answer: what was checked, what wasn't, and why.

What blueHealthcare is

An overlay that sits above the systems already in place — the EHR, the payer stack, the LIMS — and produces a signed, offline-verifiable receipt of what a review actually checked. It never replaces the EHR, the payer system, or clinical authority. A named human remains the only decision authority for every product in the suite.

"None found" is not the same as "I could not look"

The suite's shared mechanism is a typed coverage ledger: every requirement or evidence gap is marked searched and absent, never searched, or undetectable with these records — never one undifferentiated "not found." A denial, a refused license, or a missed error class can be checked against what the review actually had the ability to see.

Where blueHealthcare is today

Built and demo-ready: blueChart and blueClaims, both with full test suites, a working UI, and a standalone offline verifier.

In active build: the five lab-integrity modules — real engines and demos exist for each; see each product's own page for what's measured today.

Not yet true, stated plainly: no pilot is signed, no live PHI has been processed, and there is no non-US or community-edition deployment.

Receipts prove process, not truth. A signed receipt proves what a blueHealthcare product ran and recorded — it does not prove that an underlying real-world claim, conclusion, or coverage determination is true. No blueHealthcare product approves, denies, recommends, pays, retracts, or alters anything.
