blueOpenHealthcare

blueOpenHealthcare is the free edition of blueHealthcare for clinics and developers in countries that are not generally considered industrialized, so that health systems that cannot pay for software can still run a signed, verifiable clinical record — and so that the people who know their country's coding, privacy and language can teach us. The licence and contribution terms are being decided by counsel; code contributions open when they are published. Until then, this is a place to read the interfaces, tell us what your jurisdiction needs, and argue with the design. No patient data, ever — real or "anonymised" — anywhere in this community.

Who this is for

blueOpenHealthcare is for developers and clinics in countries that are not generally considered industrialized — free to use, provided you share back what you build. The exact test is the World Bank's country income classification: low- and lower-middle-income countries are eligible automatically, upper-middle-income countries may apply case by case, and high-income countries (Canada, the US, the UK, Germany, Australia, Saudi Arabia among them) need a commercial licence. Anyone, anywhere, may read the code and build with synthetic data — the location test applies to running it for real patients. Share-back means registering each live clinic and sending back the code of your changes and translations — never patient data. See the full eligibility and share-back terms.

Canada, the US, Europe, and other high-income countries: contact us for a commercial licence.

What's in the free edition

A lean list, expanded as it goes:

  • Included now: patient record and terminology, registration and scheduling, clinical notes, the launcher, the receipt verifier (open to everyone), and a developer kit — synthetic demo data, a sandbox, and translation and country templates.
  • Planned, added as we go: orders, results, medicines, lab, imaging, integrations.
  • Not in the free edition: the eight commercial products listed on the healthcare overview. Organisations in eligible countries can ask for any of them free, case by case.
