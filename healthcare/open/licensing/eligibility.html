Country eligibility and share-back terms

These are drafts prepared for counsel. They are not in effect, and no licence has been granted.

This is the current direction for who can use blueOpenHealthcare and on what terms: free by the World Bank's own published country-income classification, provided they share back what they build. It refines the licensing options memo's recommendation, which did not include a country limit.

Source-available, not open source

An open-source licence, by the Open Source Initiative's own definition, cannot discriminate against a person, group, or field of use — including by country. A country-limited licence therefore cannot be called "open source" and cannot use an OSI-approved licence such as MPL, Apache, or GPL for the parts it limits. This is still called blueOpenHealthcare, and described as free for eligible countries or source-available.

Who's eligible

Eligibility uses the World Bank's country income classification, published annually — an official, public list already used by lawyers and funders, rather than a bespoke cut-off.

  • Low- and lower-middle-income countries are eligible automatically.
  • Upper-middle-income countries may apply; MOBLUEHQ decides case by case.
  • High-income countries need a commercial licence.
  • Sanctioned countries are excluded regardless of income group.

Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, Australia, and Saudi Arabia are all high-income — every market where blueHealthcare is sold commercially is excluded from the free edition by the same test.

The test is where the patients and clinics are, not where the developer lives. Anyone, anywhere, may read the code, study it, and build with synthetic data. Only production use for patients has a location test.

This programme exists for two reasons, not one. The first is that health systems that cannot pay for software can still run a signed, verifiable clinical record. The second: it is a distribution channel to developers and clinicians who already move between two health systems — much of blueHealthcare's own workforce trained or worked in the health systems this programme serves, and the free edition is how we reach them and let them build with us, not only pay us. Both are real purposes; neither is the whole story on its own.

Share-back

As a condition of this licence, a deploying organisation must:

  • Register each production deployment with MOBLUEHQ — organisation name, country, contact, and which modules are in use — before or within 30 days of going live.
  • Send back the code of any modification, translation, or extension it distributes or puts into production, under these same terms, with a short description of what it does. MOBLUEHQ may incorporate it into the software and its commercial products.
Share-back never means patient data. Nothing in this licence requires or permits sending MOBLUEHQ any patient data, personal data, or health information. Share-back covers code, configuration, translations, and descriptions only.
A country that graduates

If an eligible country is later reclassified as high-income, clinics already registered there may continue under these terms for three years, after which continued production use needs a commercial licence.

Everyone else

Any use not covered above — a high-income country, or a use outside "Permitted Use" — needs a commercial licence from MOBLUEHQ. Contact us.

What the free edition includes

A lean list, decided now, expanded as it goes:

  • Included now: patient record and terminology, registration and scheduling, clinical notes, the launcher, the receipt verifier (open to everyone), and a developer kit — synthetic demo data, a sandbox, and translation and country templates.
  • Planned, added as we go: orders, results, medicines, lab, imaging, integrations.
  • Not in the free edition: the commercial products listed on the healthcare overview. Organisations in eligible countries can ask for any of them free, case by case.

See the open overview and the planned downloads for how this reaches a clinic.

Before any of this is published

A short list of security prerequisites has to be true first — a clean repository, closed care-team access gaps, keys that fail closed outside demo mode, and a disclosure process. See the security page for the full list.

Who runs this

MOBLUEHQ, Inc. runs the programme today. Contributor terms are written so that rights can pass to a non-profit foundation later, if one is formed. See governance.

Open questions for counsel
  • Is "not high-income under the World Bank classification on the date of first use" clear enough to enforce, and which snapshot date governs?
  • Is "patients located in an eligible country" workable for cross-border hosting and telehealth?
  • Is mandatory share-back of modifications enforceable across these jurisdictions — a licence condition, or a separate contractual covenant?
  • Does registering a deployment's contact details create any data-protection obligation for MOBLUEHQ?
  • Can the blueOpenHealthcare and "blue…" marks be licensed only to compliant users, as the practical enforcement lever?
  • What must contributor terms and trademark ownership look like today so a later transfer to a non-profit foundation is clean?
